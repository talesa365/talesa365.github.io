The Story

The sound of the heavy metal door closing brought it all back. The cold metal bars, the fences lined with razor wire, the women yelling, the officers’ keys jangling – these are the sounds of a prison sentence. I was inmate #000006 with the Department of Correction, broken, and battered. I had spent most of my life in poverty struggling with domestic violence and addiction. I never expected that in this chaos and concrete I would be rebuilt. In 2018, I was introduced to A Web Development Program inside the walls, which equips participants with in-demand technology skills. I was 1 of 6 women selected for the first cohort of this pilot program. I had no prior experience with web development and no access to the internet, yet I learned to build full stack applications from start to finish. After pushing myself, embracing and overcoming challenges, and continually striving to support and uplift my fellow classmates, I graduated at the top of my class with my capstone chosen to present to the Workforce Advisory Board including the President and CEO of Walmart and Apple. Six weeks after graduating, I was released. Daily I battled the imposter syndrome, and still do. Yet, I was determined to go after the career I had been dreaming of inside that coding program. It happened, I was offered and accepted a job as the Systems Analyst & Webmaster with that same Department of Correction.


The growing integration of data science with artificial intelligence and machine learning has led to an increasing demand for data science professionals who can handle complex datasets and build predictive models. Companies are seeking candidates with strong programming and analytical skills, leading to new job titles like machine learning engineers, AI architects, and big data analysts.

73% of companies that implement data storytelling report better decision-making processes (source: Forbes Insights). Data storytelling increases understanding of complex datasets by up to 80%, as it adds context and emotion to raw figures.

Over 40% of career changers into the tech industry are aged 40 or older (source: Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2022).
Despite the growing trend, 36% of workers over 40 reported facing age discrimination when applying for tech jobs (source: AARP 2022). It’s projected that by 2025, 30% of new hires in the tech industry could be people over 40, as more professionals seek job stability and better pay in tech-related roles (source: CompTIA)


The Challenge

As I built applications and navigated through coding challenges, I discovered the immense value of data in telling stories. Data science, for me, is about uncovering stories within raw numbers, much like my personal story—finding meaning in adversity. While my first step into technology was through web development, my long-term vision has evolved toward data science. This career offers the perfect blend of technical skill and creativity. The challenge of interpreting data, coupled with the opportunity to apply these insights to meaningful problems, excites me. Data science is the bridge between the abstract world of information and the tangible impact it can have on the world around us. This is where I see myself making a difference. The challenge of returning to school and learning data science later in life has been a real struggle, but also empowering. I am doing my best at adapting to the rigorous demands of work and school. I’ve found that storytelling through data offers me a creative outlet and a way to make meaningful contributions. Age and background are obstacles, but they are also what make my perspective unique.For me, a dream job would be one where I can use data to tell stories that make an impact. My long-term goal is to work in a role that allows me to bridge the gap between abstract data and real-world impact.
